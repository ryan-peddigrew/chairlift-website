Example · Home services

The after-hours inquiry

A quote request comes in through the website at 8:40 pm. The office is closed.

By the time anyone reads it the next morning, the customer has already booked someone who answered first.

Before

  1. The form lands in a shared inbox.
  2. Nobody sees it until the next morning.
  3. The first reply goes out a day later, if someone remembers.
  4. The customer has already moved on.

After

  1. The customer gets a friendly reply within minutes, explaining what happens next.
  2. The inquiry is tagged by service and area and assigned to the right person.
  3. Anything urgent is flagged to whoever is on call.
  4. First thing in the morning, the team sees a short list of what came in overnight.
Typically built with: Your website form, your email, and the calendar or job system you already use.
Example · Trades and contractors

The quote that went quiet

A salesperson sends a detailed quote and fully intends to follow up. Then the week happens.

Two weeks later, nobody is sure whether the client ever replied.

Before

  1. The quote is emailed as a PDF.
  2. Following up depends on someone remembering.
  3. Busy weeks mean check-ins slip, or never happen.
  4. Work is lost without anyone deciding to lose it.

After

  1. Sending the quote starts a short, polite follow-up sequence.
  2. Each check-in is written in the salesperson's own voice.
  3. The sequence stops the moment the client replies.
  4. The salesperson gets a nudge when a conversation needs a person.
Typically built with: The quoting tool or template you already send from, plus your email and CRM or spreadsheet.
Example · Multi-location clinic or salon

Reviews at every location

Every location is told to ask happy customers for a review. A couple of them do.

Most forget on busy days, and nobody can see which locations are asking.

Before

  1. Asking for reviews depends on the person at the front desk.
  2. Some locations ask, most forget.
  3. Customers get the wrong link, or no link at all.
  4. Managers can't see what's being sent where.

After

  1. After each visit, a short thank-you goes out with the right review link for that location.
  2. One gentle reminder follows if it's still useful.
  3. Managers see requests by location in one place.
  4. Front-desk staff no longer have to remember to ask.
Typically built with: Your booking or point-of-sale system, your review profiles, and text or email.
Example · Account team

When someone's out of the office

Each account manager keeps client history in their own inbox. When someone is on holiday or moves on, their clients go quiet and the next person starts from scratch.

Before

  1. Client history lives in individual inboxes.
  2. When someone's away, clients wait.
  3. A handover means forwarding dozens of emails.
  4. Follow-ups slip through the gap.

After

  1. Client conversations are logged in one shared place, without anyone retyping them.
  2. Follow-ups are scheduled from the record, not from memory.
  3. Covering for a colleague means opening the account and reading the history.
  4. Leadership can see every relationship at a glance.
Typically built with: Your shared inbox or email accounts, and the CRM or client list your team already keeps.
