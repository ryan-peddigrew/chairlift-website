Ticket 01

Blue square: end-to-end sales-to-install pipeline

Small Space Innovations

Space design, modular millwork and turnkey installation for commercial and multi-unit residential projects

  • Spec intake
  • Quote cadence
  • Job dispatch
  • Milestone updates

At the base: the friction

Plenty of inquiries, but measurements were gathered by hand, quotes went out slowly, prospects went quiet, and details slipped between design, workshop and installers.

On the lift: the system built

Four quiet systems: guided intake, timed quote follow-through, a job packet built when the deposit lands, and stage updates to the client.

At the summit: the operational result

Faster quote-to-deposit, no details dropped between design and the field, and hours of design time back each week.

Summit reached

Under the hood: system blueprint

  1. Structured spec and dimension intake. Clients submit exact room dimensions, site constraints and finish preferences before the first call.
  2. Quiet quote follow-through cadence. A timed, multi-stage track watches every open custom quote and keeps it moving toward a deposit, so designers do not chase.
  3. One-click fabrication and install dispatch. When a deposit lands, one job packet pulls together specs, site access rules and crew schedules.
  4. Proactive milestone client updates. Triggered updates go out at design sign-off, fabrication and installation day.

Ticket 02

Black diamond: multi-division routing and pipeline

Smith & Long

Commercial and industrial electrical, mechanical, high-voltage and engineering services. A Canadian enterprise with over 90 years of work behind it

  • Inquiry switchboard
  • Field relay
  • Renewal tracker
  • Tender cadence

At the base: the friction

Many divisions, long tender cycles, and upgrade chances spotted on site that never reached sales. Maintenance and compliance dates were tracked by hand.

On the lift: the system built

Four quiet systems: a switchboard for inquiries, a field-to-sales relay, a renewal tracker, and set follow-up tracks for every tender.

At the summit: the operational result

Tighter coordination across divisions, expansion work captured from the field, predictable renewals, and no tender left to go cold.

Summit reached

Under the hood: system blueprint

  1. Multi-division inquiry and tender switchboard. Commercial, institutional and industrial tenders are sorted and sent to the right trade division and account executive.
  2. Field-to-sales opportunity relay. Technicians and project managers flag aging switchgear, lighting retrofits or mechanical upgrades onto the sales board in under 60 seconds.
  3. Preventative compliance and renewal tracker. Inspection windows, arc-flash audits and maintenance intervals are tracked, and the account lead is cued 60 days before renewal.
  4. Multi-stakeholder tender cadence. Set follow-up tracks across general contractors, property managers and facility directors keep six- and seven-figure bids moving.

Example · Home services

The after-hours inquiry

A quote request comes in through the website at 8:40 pm. The office is closed. By the time anyone reads it the next morning, the customer has already booked someone who answered first.

Before

  1. The form lands in a shared inbox.
  2. Nobody sees it until the next morning.
  3. The first reply goes out a day later, if someone remembers.
  4. The customer has already moved on.

After

  1. The customer gets a friendly reply within minutes, explaining what happens next.
  2. The inquiry is tagged by service and area and assigned to the right person.
  3. Anything urgent is flagged to whoever is on call.
  4. First thing in the morning, the team sees a short list of what came in overnight.
Typically built with: Your website form, your email, and the calendar or job system you already use.
Example · Trades and contractors

The quote that went quiet

A salesperson sends a detailed quote and fully intends to follow up. Then the week happens. Two weeks later, nobody is sure whether the client ever replied.

Before

  1. The quote is emailed as a PDF.
  2. Following up depends on someone remembering.
  3. Busy weeks mean check-ins slip, or never happen.
  4. Work is lost without anyone deciding to lose it.

After

  1. Sending the quote starts a short, polite follow-up sequence.
  2. Each check-in is written in the salesperson's own voice.
  3. The sequence stops the moment the client replies.
  4. The salesperson gets a nudge when a conversation needs a person.
Typically built with: The quoting tool or template you already send from, plus your email and CRM or spreadsheet.
Example · Multi-location clinic or salon

Reviews at every location

Every location is told to ask happy customers for a review. A couple of them do. Most forget on busy days, and nobody can see which locations are asking.

Before

  1. Asking for reviews depends on the person at the front desk.
  2. Some locations ask, most forget.
  3. Customers get the wrong link, or no link at all.
  4. Managers can't see what's being sent where.

After

  1. After each visit, a short thank-you goes out with the right review link for that location.
  2. One gentle reminder follows if it's still useful.
  3. Managers see requests by location in one place.
  4. Front-desk staff no longer have to remember to ask.
Typically built with: Your booking or point-of-sale system, your review profiles, and text or email.
Example · Account team

When someone's out of the office

Each account manager keeps client history in their own inbox. When someone is on holiday or moves on, their clients go quiet and the next person starts from scratch.

Before

  1. Client history lives in individual inboxes.
  2. When someone's away, clients wait.
  3. A handover means forwarding dozens of emails.
  4. Follow-ups slip through the gap.

After

  1. Client conversations are logged in one shared place, without anyone retyping them.
  2. Follow-ups are scheduled from the record, not from memory.
  3. Covering for a colleague means opening the account and reading the history.
  4. Leadership can see every relationship at a glance.
Typically built with: Your shared inbox or email accounts, and the CRM or client list your team already keeps.
